# Homemade HD Projector

Built this back in 2015.

🌐 **[View the project page](https://bhaviksapru.github.io/projector/)**

---

## What it is

An LCD projector built from scratch. Light from a 400W metal halide bulb passes through a condenser system, through a 17" Dell LCD panel, and out through an antique Cooke triplet lens onto a wall or screen. The whole thing sits in a hand-cut wooden enclosure with active cooling, UV/IR filtration, and a custom ignition circuit for the lamp.

Projected onto a 152-inch diagonal screen at 14 feet. Looks great in a dark room.

---

## The optics

The optical path was the hardest part to figure out. The antique cooke triplet was sourced from an antique supplier in washington. These were made during WorldWarII. Lens with similar specifications are available today but are prohibitively expensive. 
Remaining items were sourced locally or from china.
Everything has to line up precisely or you get a dim, blurry mess.

| Component | Spec |
|---|---|
| Projection lens | Antique Cooke Triplet, 18" EF (Charles Beseler) |
| Field lens | 550mm focal length |
| LCD panel | 17" Dell — 12ms, 500:1 contrast, 5:4 aspect ratio |
| Condenser lens | 330mm focal length |
| Collimator | 232mm FL, 92mm dia |
| Bulb | 400W metal halide, 5500K, 30,000 lumens, 27mm short arc |
| Reflector | 68mm dia, 17.3mm FL |
| Fans | PC fans, 120 CFM |

---

## Build process

Nothing fancy here — just planning, cutting, assembling, and a lot of iterating.

1. Drew up a plan with all the optical distances worked out
2. Cut and assembled the enclosure from slates
3. Fitted the Cooke triplet lens at the front
4. Built the internal foundation to mount everything
5. Wired up the electricals — transformer, capacitor, bulb ignitor
6. Mounted the 400W lamp and reflector
7. Spent a good while aligning the optics
8. Fitted the LCD panel and its controller board
9. Added filtered air intake and exhaust fans
10. Sealed it up, painted it, done

---

## How it performs

Works well in a dark room. Colors are good, image is sharp once the optics are dialled in. There's some light leakage during the day but that's mostly a sealing issue — easy enough to fix with heavy curtains in all of the windows.

The 400W lamp runs hot, so the cooling system matters. The filtered intake + exhaust setup keeps things stable.

---

## Notes

- Getting the collimator distance right relative to the reflector took the most trial and error
- The LCD panel needs its backlight removed — the projector lamp replaces it
- UV and IR filters are important; without them the lamp will degrade the LCD over time
- Image is double inverted. (hence correct on screen)
